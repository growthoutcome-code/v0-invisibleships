# Why “Invisible Ships”

*Shown to every reader on the way into the archive. It is the archive's
account of its own name and its own premise.*

## Perceptual set

Perceptual set is a well-documented principle in cognitive psychology: our expectations, prior experience, context, and mental frameworks shape what we perceive — and can keep us from registering something we have no concept for. We tend to notice what we are prepared to notice.

The work takes its name from an old, often-told story. When European ships first appeared off certain coastlines, it is said the people onshore barely reacted — the vessels lay so far outside anything they had seen that they struggled to register them at all, until someone who studied the strange disturbance on the water learned to see them, and taught others where to look.

**The anecdote is almost certainly apocryphal. The principle it dramatizes is not — perceptual set is real and measurable.**

This archive is an attempt to make something visible: to describe, in plain and dated detail, a phenomenon that is easy to look past precisely because most people have no framework for it yet.

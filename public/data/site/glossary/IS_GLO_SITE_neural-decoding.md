# neural decoding

*Site-authored. This term was written for invisibleships.com to explain the work; it is not extracted from the source document series, and it carries no source-document id for that reason. Terms drawn from the primary record are in `glossary/` and do carry one.*


Using software, increasingly machine learning, to turn recorded brain signals into information: an intended movement, a word, an image, or an estimate of activity the device cannot directly measure.

Decoding is what makes a [brain–computer interface](/glossary/braincomputer-interface-bci) useful. It is also where the questions of [cognitive liberty](/glossary/cognitive-liberty) begin, because the better the decoding, the more a recording can reveal.

Documented real-world example: in the UC San Diego study behind [transparent brain-computer interfaces](/glossary/transparent-brain-computer-interfaces-bcis), a neural network called a BiLSTM (bidirectional long short-term memory), which is built to handle signals that change over time, learned to predict deep [calcium imaging](/glossary/calcium-imaging) activity in mice from surface electrical recordings alone. The authors reported it could predict both average activity and the activity of single cells.

Related terms: [artificial intelligence](/glossary/artificial-intelligence), [software](/glossary/software), [brain–computer interface](/glossary/braincomputer-interface-bci), [calcium imaging](/glossary/calcium-imaging), [cognitive liberty](/glossary/cognitive-liberty)

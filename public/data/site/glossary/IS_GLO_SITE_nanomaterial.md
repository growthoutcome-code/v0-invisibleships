# nanomaterial

*Site-authored. This term was written for invisibleships.com to explain the work; it is not extracted from the source document series, and it carries no source-document id for that reason. Terms drawn from the primary record are in `glossary/` and do carry one.*


Any material with at least one dimension in the nanoscale range, roughly 1 to 100 nanometres. Examples include [graphene](/glossary/graphene), which is one atom thick; carbon nanotubes; nanowires; and [nanoparticles](/glossary/nanoparticle).

In neurotechnology, nanomaterials are used to make the parts of a device that touch the brain thinner, more flexible and closer to the softness of tissue, and in some cases transparent. The aim is a device the body tolerates for longer and that records more cleanly.

Documented real-world example: the [transparent brain-computer interfaces](/glossary/transparent-brain-computer-interfaces-bcis) built at UC San Diego use a graphene electrode array thin and clear enough for a microscope to see through it.

Related terms: [nanotechnology](/glossary/nanotechnology), [graphene](/glossary/graphene), [injectable mesh electronics](/glossary/injectable-mesh-electronics), [transparent brain-computer interfaces](/glossary/transparent-brain-computer-interfaces-bcis)

Sources: [Just How Small Is Nano? — U.S. National Nanotechnology Initiative](https://www.nano.gov/about-nanotechnology/just-how-small-is-nano); [Transparent Brain-Computer Interface Uses AI and Nanotech — Psychology Today (Cami Rosso, 24 Jan 2024)](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-future-brain/202401/transparent-brain-computer-interface-uses-ai-and-nanotech)

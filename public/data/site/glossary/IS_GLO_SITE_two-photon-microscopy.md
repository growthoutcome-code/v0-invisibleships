# two-photon microscopy

*Site-authored. This term was written for invisibleships.com to explain the work; it is not extracted from the source document series, and it carries no source-document id for that reason. Terms drawn from the primary record are in `glossary/` and do carry one.*


A laser microscope that can image living tissue up to a fraction of a millimetre below the surface with little damage. It works by making fluorescent markers glow only at the exact point where the laser is focused, which keeps the image sharp at depth.

In neuroscience it is used to watch individual neurons at work, almost always in laboratory animals fitted with a small window in the skull. It needs the animal to stay under the microscope, which limits how long and how freely it can be observed.

Documented real-world example: in the UC San Diego study behind [transparent brain-computer interfaces](/glossary/transparent-brain-computer-interfaces-bcis), a two-photon microscope imaged neurons up to 250 micrometres deep, shining its laser through the transparent [graphene](/glossary/graphene) array.

# nanotechnology

*Site-authored. This term was written for invisibleships.com to explain the work; it is not extracted from the source document series, and it carries no source-document id for that reason. Terms drawn from the primary record are in `glossary/` and do carry one.*


The science and engineering of materials and devices at the scale of roughly 1 to 100 nanometres. A nanometre is one billionth of a metre. A sheet of paper is about 100,000 nanometres thick.

At this scale materials can behave differently from the same material in bulk: stronger, more conductive, more reactive, or see-through. That is why nanotechnology turns up in electronics, medicine and neuroscience alike.

In medicine, its most widely used product so far is the [lipid nanoparticle](/glossary/lipid-nanoparticle) that carries mRNA in the Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna COVID-19 vaccines. In neurotechnology, it is used to make electrodes thinner, softer, wireless or transparent, so that they disturb brain tissue less. The best-known example on this site is the [transparent brain-computer interfaces](/glossary/transparent-brain-computer-interfaces-bcis).

Related terms: [nanomaterial](/glossary/nanomaterial), [nanoparticle](/glossary/nanoparticle), [nanomedicine](/glossary/nanomedicine), [neural interface](/glossary/neural-interface), [transparent brain-computer interfaces](/glossary/transparent-brain-computer-interfaces-bcis)

Sources: [Just How Small Is Nano? — U.S. National Nanotechnology Initiative](https://www.nano.gov/about-nanotechnology/just-how-small-is-nano); [Transparent Brain-Computer Interface Uses AI and Nanotech — Psychology Today (Cami Rosso, 24 Jan 2024)](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-future-brain/202401/transparent-brain-computer-interface-uses-ai-and-nanotech)

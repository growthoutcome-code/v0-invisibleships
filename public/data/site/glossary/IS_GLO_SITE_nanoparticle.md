# nanoparticle

*Site-authored. This term was written for invisibleships.com to explain the work; it is not extracted from the source document series, and it carries no source-document id for that reason. Terms drawn from the primary record are in `glossary/` and do carry one.*


A particle roughly 1 to 100 nanometres across. Nanoparticles can be made from fats, metals, polymers or ceramics, depending on the job.

In medicine they are mostly used as carriers: they package a drug or genetic material so it survives the trip through the body and reaches the right cells. In neuroscience research they are also being studied as a way to stimulate brain cells from outside the head, using magnetic fields or light that passes through the skull.

Documented real-world example: the mRNA COVID-19 vaccines from Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna each use a [lipid nanoparticle](/glossary/lipid-nanoparticle) to carry the mRNA into cells.

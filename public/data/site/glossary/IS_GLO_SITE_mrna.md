# mRNA (messenger RNA)

*Site-authored. This term was written for invisibleships.com to explain the work; it is not extracted from the source document series, and it carries no source-document id for that reason. Terms drawn from the primary record are in `glossary/` and do carry one.*


A molecule that carries instructions from a cell’s DNA to the machinery that builds proteins.

An mRNA vaccine delivers instructions for one protein from a virus (for COVID-19, the spike protein). The cell builds that protein for a short time, and the immune system learns to recognise it. The mRNA does not enter the cell nucleus, where DNA is kept, and it breaks down after use.

Because mRNA is fragile, it has to be packaged for delivery. In the COVID-19 vaccines, that package is a [lipid nanoparticle](/glossary/lipid-nanoparticle).

Related terms: [lipid nanoparticle](/glossary/lipid-nanoparticle), [nanomedicine](/glossary/nanomedicine)

Sources: [What are the ingredients in the COVID-19 mRNA vaccines? — Vaccine Education Center, Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia](https://www.chop.edu/centers-programs/vaccine-education-center/video/what-are-the-different-ingredients-being-used-in-the-covid-19-mrna-vaccines)

# graphene

*Site-authored. This term was written for invisibleships.com to explain the work; it is not extracted from the source document series, and it carries no source-document id for that reason. Terms drawn from the primary record are in `glossary/` and do carry one.*


A single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a honeycomb pattern. It is one atom thick, very strong, flexible, conducts electricity well, and lets almost all light through.

Those properties are why researchers use it for brain electrodes. A graphene array can record electrical signals from the surface of the brain while a microscope looks straight through it at the tissue beneath. That is the idea behind [transparent brain-computer interfaces](/glossary/transparent-brain-computer-interfaces-bcis).

Graphene was first isolated in 2004. Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov received the 2010 Nobel Prize in Physics for it.

Graphene oxide is a different, chemically altered form, studied separately for uses such as drug delivery. The two names are often confused.

Related terms: [nanomaterial](/glossary/nanomaterial), [electrocorticography](/glossary/electrocorticography), [transparent brain-computer interfaces](/glossary/transparent-brain-computer-interfaces-bcis), [lipid nanoparticle](/glossary/lipid-nanoparticle)

# neural interface

*Site-authored. This term was written for invisibleships.com to explain the work; it is not extracted from the source document series, and it carries no source-document id for that reason. Terms drawn from the primary record are in `glossary/` and do carry one.*


Any device that records signals from the nervous system, sends signals into it, or both. A [brain–computer interface](/glossary/braincomputer-interface-bci) is one kind: a neural interface connected to a computer.

Neural interfaces range from caps worn on the scalp, such as the [electroencephalogram](/glossary/electroencephalogram), to sheets of electrodes placed on the brain’s surface ([electrocorticography](/glossary/electrocorticography)) and implants that go into brain tissue. The closer a device sits to the neurons, the clearer the signal, and the more surgery it needs.

Much current research, including nanotechnology, aims to break that trade-off: to get a clear signal with a device that is smaller, softer, wireless or less invasive.

Related terms: [brain–computer interface](/glossary/braincomputer-interface-bci), [hardware](/glossary/hardware-computer), [electrocorticography](/glossary/electrocorticography), [neural dust](/glossary/neural-dust), [injectable mesh electronics](/glossary/injectable-mesh-electronics), [neural decoding](/glossary/neural-decoding)

## What this chart shows

- [A] Police-recorded violent crime fell 30% from 1999 to 2019, then a further 16% from 2020 to 2025 on the FBI's newer count.
- [A] Property crime fell further: 44% from 1999 to 2019, then 20% from 2020 to 2025.
- [A] Violence reported by victims in the national survey, which includes crimes never reported to police, is about half its 1999 level.
- [A] The survey jumped from its 2021 low — 16.5 to 23.5 per 1,000 people in 2022 — and has stayed there, while police-recorded violence has fallen since 2022.
- [A] The police lines break at 2020 because the FBI changed how it counts. 2021 is left out: the FBI says that year is not nationally representative.

## The series

### Violent crime (police records)
FBI Uniform Crime Reporting Program · tier A · 26 points, 1999–2025

- First: 100 (1999) · Last: 62.6 (2025)
- Highest: 100 (1999) · Lowest: 62.6 (2025)
- Basis: FBI violent crime rate per 100,000; indexed to 1999 = 100
- Caveat: 1999: Crime in the United States 2010, Table 1. 2000-2019: Crime in the United States 2019, Table 1, whose violent total uses the legacy rape definition for every year.
- Caveat: 2020 onward: UCR Summary of Reported Crimes in the Nation, 2025, Figure 1. That report prints the total only for 2024 (362.9) and 2025 (327.6); 2020, 2022 and 2023 are the sum of its four published offence rates, a method that reproduces the two printed totals exactly.
- Caveat: Not joined at 2020. The FBI moved from its Summary Reporting System to NIBRS-based national estimates, and the 2020-onward figures come from a later report that re-estimated those years; 2019 and 2020 are not one measurement. The FBI calls the two 'analogous measures rather than as one being more accurate than the other.'
- Caveat: 2021 is not drawn: the FBI states its 2021 estimates are 'below a statistically acceptable level to be nationally representative and are not comparable to other yearly estimates.'

### Violence reported by victims (survey)
Bureau of Justice Statistics · tier A · 25 points, 1999–2024

- First: 100 (1999) · Last: 49.4 (2024)
- Highest: 100 (1999) · Lowest: 34.7 (2020)
- Basis: BJS violent victimization rate per 1,000 persons 12+; indexed to 1999 = 100
- Caveat: Asks a national sample of households whether they were victims, so it counts crimes the police never hear about. Includes simple assault, which the FBI's violent total does not.
- Caveat: 1999-2012 and 2024: Criminal Victimization, 2024, appendix table 1. 2013-2023: Criminal Victimization, 2023, appendix table 1. The two editions agree where they overlap.
- Caveat: 2006 is not drawn: BJS states that 'estimates for 2006 should not be compared to other years.'
- Caveat: A survey estimate with a margin of error: BJS prints a standard error and a 95% confidence interval for every year, so small year-to-year moves may not be real.

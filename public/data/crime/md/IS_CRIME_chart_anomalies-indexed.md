**Unit.** Each lane indexed to its own first year = 100 — direction only, never size · **Publisher.** FBI NCIC; Adult Psychiatric Morbidity Survey; Chapman University; AARO / ODNI · **Evidence tier.** A

## What this chart shows

- [A] Missing-person records are at their modern low, about a fifth below 2014.
- [A] Whether that is fewer people going missing or fewer records being entered cannot be separated.
- [A] Home invasion has no lane here, because no country counts it as an offence. It is filed as burglary, and burglary has fallen for twenty years.
- [A] In England, adults reporting a psychotic symptom in the past year went from 5.6% to 6.8% between 2000 and 2014. Then the survey stopped publishing it.
- [A] No United States federal survey asks about hallucinations at all.
- [A] The haunting line measures belief, not experience — what people think is possible, not what has happened to them.
- [A] Gallup has asked Americans whether they believe in ghosts since 1990, and has never once asked whether they have seen one.
- [A] Pew asked the experience question twice, then declared its own two readings incomparable after moving from telephone to online.
- [A] Reports to the federal UAP office rose sharply, then fell.
- [A] The office attributes the rise to reduced stigma and to the FAA starting to forward reports weekly — a reporting system being built, not the sky changing.
- [A] Four lanes, four different things: records entered, symptoms reported, beliefs held, reports received.
- [A] They are drawn together because each is what exists — not because any one corroborates another. Nothing here establishes a relationship.

## The series

### Missing-person records
FBI NCIC · tier B · 11 points, 2014–2025

- First: 100 (2014) · Last: 78.5 (2025)
- Highest: 102.7 (2017) · Lowest: 78.5 (2025)
- Basis: NCIC records entered per year; indexed to 2014 = 100
- Caveat: Counts RECORDS, not people: one person can generate more than one, and a record is cancelled when a case closes for any reason — cancellation does not mean found alive.
- Caveat: Adults are not subject to the same mandatory-entry rules as juveniles, so adult cases are under-entered by an unknown margin.

### Psychotic symptoms (England)
APMS · tier A · 3 points, 2000–2014

- First: 100 (2000) · Last: 121.4 (2014)
- Highest: 121.4 (2014) · Lowest: 100 (2000)
- Basis: adults screening positive in the past year; indexed to 2000 = 100
- Caveat: ENGLAND ONLY, ages 16–74. There is no equivalent United States series: no federal survey asks all respondents about hallucinations or voice-hearing at all.
- Caveat: THE QUESTION STOPPED BEING PUBLISHED. The 2023/24 survey still uses the screening instrument but no longer reports the screen-positive rate as an outcome, so the series ends in 2014 and cannot be extended.
- Caveat: Response rate fell from just under 70% in 2000 to 57% in 2007 and 2014. On a question about a stigmatised experience, that is a live confounder.
- Caveat: The published figure is 'any psychotic symptom', which folds hallucinations in with paranoia and thought interference. No item-level breakdown is published, so hallucinations cannot be separated out.

### Belief that places can be haunted
Chapman University · tier B · 4 points, 2016–2021

- First: 100 (2016) · Last: 113.1 (2021)
- Highest: 123.8 (2018) · Lowest: 100 (2016)
- Basis: percent agreeing, US adults; indexed to 2016 = 100
- Caveat: THIS IS BELIEF, NOT EXPERIENCE. The item is agreement with the statement 'places can be haunted by spirits'. It measures what people think is possible, not what has happened to them.
- Caveat: The 2019 wave exists but published only subgroup figures, so 2019 is a genuine gap rather than a smoothed line.
- Caveat: Online panel. No paranormal results have been published since the January 2021 wave, though the survey itself continues.

### UAP reports received
AARO / ODNI · tier A · 4 points, 2022–2025

- First: 100 (2022) · Last: 129.1 (2025)
- Highest: 306.5 (2024) · Lowest: 100 (2022)
- Basis: reports received by AARO per reporting period; indexed to 2022 = 100
- Caveat: THIS IS AN INTAKE COUNT, NOT A RATE. The reporting periods are 18 months, 8 months, 13 months and 12 months — unequal, and not aligned to calendar years. The 2021 figure of 144 covers SEVENTEEN years and is excluded from this lane for that reason.
- Caveat: The counts mix in-period events with back-reports of older ones: 272 of the 757 in the FY2024 report were events from 2021–2022.
- Caveat: AARO AND ODNI ATTRIBUTE THE RISE TO REPORTING, NOT TO EVENTS. ODNI records it as 'partially due to reduced stigma surrounding UAP reporting'; AARO records that the FAA began forwarding reports weekly, 'a significant increase from the previous reporting period'. This lane is a picture of a reporting office being built.
- Caveat: AARO also records a collection bias favouring the continental United States and US military operational areas, and diagnosed one of its own prior-year distributions as an artefact of a batch of FAA paperwork.

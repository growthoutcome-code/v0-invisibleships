## What this chart shows

- [A] Detention has a shape: a V, then a spike. A 50,165 average in FY2019, down 61% to 19,461 in FY2021, back to 37,721 by FY2024.
- [A] Then the 2025–26 surge, which is visible only in single-day counts.
- [A] Is it overcrowded? The funded ceiling is the answer. Congress funds 41,500 beds for FY2026 — 'thousands fewer than the current level', in the bill's own words.
- [A] Single-day counts ran 65,000 to 73,000, because $45 billion arrived outside the annual bed line in July 2025.
- [B] National utilisation hides local overcrowding. On 13 April 2025 the system sat at 76% of contracted beds, yet 45 of 181 facilities were over their own contract that night.
- [B] Most people held have no criminal conviction — 70.6% on 11 July 2026 — and 92% of FY2026's growth was people with none.
- [A] The average daily population line stops at FY2024 because no later average has been published. That gap is drawn as a gap.

## Note on reading this

Three different measures are drawn here and they must not be read as one line. The solid line is AVERAGE DAILY POPULATION by fiscal year — ICE's own published figure, and it stops at FY2024 because no FY2025 or FY2026 average has been published. The stepped line is the detention beds Congress FUNDS. The separate points are SINGLE-DAY counts during the 2025–26 surge: a snapshot, not an average, which is why they sit above any plausible annual mean for those years. Conflating the three is the most common error in reporting on this subject.

This chart draws three measures that are constantly conflated in reporting on this subject: an annual average, a funded ceiling, and single-day snapshots. Each is labelled with which it is. Figures for 2025–26 come from FOIA-derived and research compilations because DHS stopped publishing detailed enforcement tables after November 2024.

This chart uses its own 2019–2026 window rather than the section's shared 1999–2025 one: the series does not exist before 2019 in comparable form, and on the shared axis four-fifths of the chart would be empty.

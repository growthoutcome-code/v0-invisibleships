## What this addendum is, and the line it holds

You asked whether *Zersetzung* tactics — the Stasi doctrine of covert psychological
"decomposition" of a targeted person by an institution — show up in litigation. The answer is
yes, but only in a specific, disciplined sense, and this addendum is built to preserve that
discipline. Every row is a **court-proven or formally-settled** instance of organized,
targeted harassment, covert surveillance, or infiltration against *named* targets. It is kept
in its own category, explicitly separate from the government-cloud cases, so it is never
conflated with them.

The anchor is **eBay v. Steiner** (D. Mass.): eBay security staff ran a cyberstalking
campaign against the journalists behind *EcommerceBytes* — a bloody pig mask, live insects, a
funeral wreath, covert surveillance, a planned GPS tracker — for which seven people were
criminally convicted, eBay paid a $3M fine, and the civil case settled for $55.7M in July
2026. Around it sit the genuine parallels: **HP's boardroom pretexting** (California AG
charges; $14.5M settlement), **Nestlé/Securitas** infiltrating Attac Switzerland (Swiss court
found it unlawful), the **Socialist Workers Party v. Attorney General** COINTELPRO judgment
(the FBI held liable for surveillance and disruption of a lawful political party — the classic
*state* Zersetzung case adjudicated in US court), Scientology's **Operation Snow White / Fair
Game** (eleven officials convicted), the UK **undercover-policing "spy cops"** scandal (Met
apology, IPT rights-breach findings, ongoing public inquiry), **WhatsApp v. NSO Group**
(Pegasus spyware against journalists and activists; jury verdict and permanent injunction),
and two matters carried with an explicit **lawfare** label — **Energy Transfer v. Greenpeace**
(a SLAPP that used litigation itself to try to financially decompose an organization) and the
contested **Chevron v. Donziger** (flagged because the court findings ran *against* Donziger,
so the "harassment" characterization is contested, not court-endorsed).

## The distinction that protects the work

This is the important part, and it's the same evidence discipline that runs through the whole
report. These cases prove that **organized, institutional harassment of targeted individuals
demonstrably occurs** — it is real, and it is sometimes provable to a criminal or civil
standard. What they do **not** do is establish the generalized "gangstalking / targeted
individual" narrative, which asserts distributed, community-wide persecution but
characteristically lacks identifiable actors, verifiable acts, or any court or regulator
finding, and which clinical and forensic literature treats as unsubstantiated. One advocacy
source explicitly frames eBay/Steiner as "gangstalking proven in court" — but the court found
that *specific, named individuals* harassed *specific victims* and left an evidentiary trail
(badges, expense reports, IP logs, testimony). That is precisely what distinguishes a proven
case from an unprovable framing. Conflating the two dilutes the credibility of documented
victims; keeping them apart is what lets this addendum stand up to a hostile reader.

So the category is labeled, bounded, and Tier-A: it belongs in the report as documented
history and precedent, and it is explicitly *not* offered as proof of any broader claim.

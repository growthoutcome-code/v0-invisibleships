## Read this first: three kinds of number, never summed

This section deliberately separates three things the market conflates. **Market sizing** is
third-party analyst TAM/forecast — useful for shape, unreliable in the decimals (sovereign
cloud is put at $59B, $80B, $117B, or $572B by 2032 depending on the firm; we carry the
range, not a point). **Capital flows** are actual dollars moving — and even here the split
matters enormously: **$1.46T is disclosed** (contract ceilings, 10-K revenue, statutory
appropriations, closed funding rounds) versus **$868B in announced pledges** (multi-year,
milestone-gated, frequently overlapping political toplines). **Thesis** is analytic judgment,
confidence-rated, with the risk stated. The single most important discipline here is refusing
to add a Gartner forecast to a White House pledge to a Palantir revenue line and call the sum
a "market."

## The macro shape

The investable story is not "government buys cloud software." It is the convergence of three
capital streams into one **sovereign-AI-compute + govtech** complex: Gulf sovereign wealth,
US hyperscaler regional capex, and US public enforcement appropriations. The most bankable
figures in the whole dataset are the disclosed ones — Palantir's Q2-2026 revenue ($1.935B,
+93% YoY; $809M US government) and ~$418B market cap; OBBBA's $170.7B statutory enforcement
appropriation; MGX's $49B fund close; and the live hyperscaler regions. The largest headline
numbers — Stargate UAE's "$500B/5GW," the $600B Saudi and $1.4T UAE political toplines — are
the least reliable, and the brief flags them as ceilings, not spend.

## By dimension

**Cross-cutting / sovereign cloud & AI-compute** — the biggest pool and the widest error
bars. Gartner sizes sovereign-cloud IaaS at ~$80B (2026) → ~$111B (2027); other firms reach
$572B by 2032 (2–3× divergence, carried as a range). Hyperscaler AI capex is ~$380B (2025,
disclosed at company level) rising to ~$675B (2026 guidance) — only a fraction government-
directed. *Thesis (moderate):* the sovereignty premium is a real, policy-driven secular trend
(the Prompt 3 localisation wave is its demand driver), but "sovereign" risks becoming
table-stakes rather than a premium, compressing margins.

**Defence-intel / govtech application layer** — the highest-conviction *near-term* read, and
the one built on disclosed rather than forecast numbers. Palantir is the proxy: exceptional,
government-anchored, disclosed revenue with OBBBA guaranteeing multi-year demand; military
cloud sized ~$14B (2026)→$34B (2031); defense-tech VC hit a record $49B in 2025 (Anduril
$2.5B at $30.5B disclosed). *Thesis (high, near-term):* valuation (~50×+ revenue) prices in
perfection; concentration in US federal/enforcement is the risk.

**Border-immigration / enforcement-tech** — the fastest-growing vertical *by public funding*.
OBBBA is the engine: $170.7B total, $45B detention, $6.2B border technology. *Thesis
(moderate return / HIGH reputational risk):* this is the domain where financial upside and
litigation/ESG/political-reversal risk diverge most sharply — the same Clearview and Nimbus
contracts that are revenue are also active lawsuit and protest surfaces (feeds directly into
open requirement #3, the litigation layer).

**Genomics-biotech** — genomics market ~$21.8B (2025)→$72.5B (2033); US federal via NHGRI
(~$664M/yr); private AI-bio (Isomorphic Labs $600M round). *Thesis (high):* the durable
government-cloud value is not sequencing but the regulated data-custody / trusted-research-
environment / compute layer — infrastructure incumbents win, and privacy regulation (EHDS,
the neural/biological-data laws from Prompt 3) is the key swing factor.

**Neurotech-BCI** — the frontier. Market only ~$2.6B (2025) but growing ~17%; the signal is
*who* is funding it: Synchron's $200M Series D **including In-Q-Tel and the Qatar Investment
Authority** (the clearest national-security-capital signal in the dataset), Neuralink (~$650M),
Merge Labs ($252M, OpenAI/Altman), against a *declining* NIH BRAIN budget ($680M→$321M as the
Cures Act top-up sunsets). *Thesis (moderate):* private capital is running ahead of
reimbursement and clearance; the government-cloud-relevant, lower-risk layer is neural-data
governance and telemetry — which is exactly where Prompt 3 found the law absent. Capital ahead
of law ahead of returns.

**Nanotech-materials** — narrow-definition market ~$1.9B but the real money is federal: the
NNI requests ~$1.45B (FY2026) on ~$47B cumulative since 2001, plus AI-for-materials startups
(Periodic Labs ~$605M). *Thesis (moderate):* government-cloud relevance is the HPC/AI-
simulation + materials-data-commons layer (DOE/NIST); long commercialization risk.

**Pandemic-response** — declining federal appetite is the finding: BARDA $1.015B (FY25)→$654M
(FY26 request), total US biodefense ~$27B FY26 and falling, with momentum shifting to the EU
(HERA) and multilateral (CEPI). *Thesis (moderate):* the durable asset is the public-health
data/surveillance infrastructure (the Prompt 4 "registries persist" finding); funding is
procyclical and receding now — a trough-timing question.

**Municipal-sensing** — bifurcating, and the split is investable. Network-effect ALPR
compounds (Flock $275M at $7.5B, >$300M ARR) while single-modality detection SaaS churns
(SoundThinking guiding ~$104M FY25, revenue *declining*). Muon tomography is contract-scale
R&D, not a VC market (Decision Sciences ~$5M DoD). *Thesis (high):* the durable value is the
multi-sensor fusion / evidence layer, not any single sensor; surveillance-regulation risk is
rising (the Prompt 1 Colorado Flock termination is the cautionary case).

**Neuro-forensics** — *no credible standalone market*; brain-fingerprinting is contested and
largely inadmissible; real spend is academic/DARPA. Any cited "market size" is marketing.
Recorded as thesis-only, low confidence — the honest finding is that there is nothing to size.

## What could not be verified / caveats

Political toplines ($600B Saudi, $200B+$1.4T UAE) are non-binding ceilings spanning all
sectors — never summed with itemised deals. Announced pledges are multi-year and milestone-
gated; annualised spend is a fraction of each headline. Market-size forecasts are analyst
products with 2–3× inter-firm variance. Several rounds are reported-not-closed (Anduril's
rumored ~$100B; MGX's $100B target vs $49B raised). The `investments` table has no time-
series depth — it is a point-in-time snapshot, not a trend.

## Call to action

1. **Anchor any investment read on the disclosed column, not the pledges.** *Finding:* $1.46T
   disclosed vs $868B pledged, and the pledges overlap political toplines. *Cost of inaction:*
   double-counting produces a fictional market.
2. **The per-dimension thesis divergence is the actionable output** — govtech (high, near-term)
   and municipal-sensing fusion (high) versus neurotech (capital-ahead-of-returns) and
   neuro-forensics (nothing to size). Allocate attention accordingly.
3. **Wire the litigation layer (open req #3) into the enforcement and neurotech theses** —
   their reputational/regulatory risk is not a footnote, it is the dominant variable, and it is
   currently uninstrumented.

**Watch, don't act:** the Gulf AI-compute megaproject dollar figures — GW capacity is more
reliable than the dollar headlines; wait for disclosed capex before treating them as real.

**Most important unanswered question:** a time-series of these flows (this is a snapshot) and
the litigation overlay — both needed before the investment thesis is decision-grade.
